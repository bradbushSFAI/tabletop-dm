# The Senn Vale: world bible

Everything in this file is common knowledge. A person who grew up in the Senn Vale knows it, or has heard it said. The DM may tell any of it to a player. The hidden truth of the world is in a different file.

## The Senn Vale in one paragraph

The Senn Vale is the northern edge of the Kingdom of Carrow: one long river valley and the hills, forest and fen around it, about six days' walk from end to end. The kingdom built roads, walls and a law here three hundred years ago, and then slowly stopped coming. The roads are still the best anyone can build. Nobody living could build them again. The people are not poor and not desperate. They farm, mine, fish and trade, they argue about tolls, and they mend what their great-grandparents made. It is a good place to live. It is also a place where something is a little off, the way a familiar room is off when one thing in it has been moved, and most people have decided not to look at it.

## Tone

- **The base note is warm.** Hearths, markets, harvest fairs, old friends and old grudges. Most people are decent, and most problems are the size of a village.
- **Under it, a wrong note.** Small things that do not add up, which people explain away. The DM never explains them either. They are felt more than seen.
- **The world is wide enough for every tone.** A comic story about a disputed sheep and a horror story about the fen both belong here, a day's walk apart. The door the player chose, and their tone answers, decide which Senn Vale they see.
- **It is a late age, not a dark one.** Things are worn, not ruined. People are tired of the crown's silence, not at war with it.

## What everyone knows about the past

- **Before the kingdom** there were the Builders. Nobody knows what they called themselves. They left the ruins on the high ground, the oldest stretches of road, and the grey milestones. Scholars in the south argue about them. People in the Senn Vale mostly use their stones for sheep pens.
- **Three hundred years ago** Carrow's legions came up the River Senn, made peace with the dwarves of the Cut and the elves of the Hushwood, and drove the goblin clans into the deep forest. They built Greywater, the river wall, and the King's Road on top of the Builders' road.
- **For two centuries** the Senn Vale paid its taxes in silver, wool and eels, and the crown sent judges, engineers and soldiers.
- **Within living memory** that stopped. The last royal judge left Greywater twenty-two years ago and no new one came. The last tax riders came nine years ago. Letters to the capital, Highcarrow, are answered late, or by a clerk, or not at all. Nothing dramatic happened. It was like a tide going out.
- **Since then** the Senn Vale governs itself, by habit and argument. Most people prefer it. Some people are afraid of what it means.

## The six places

Distances are in days on foot by road. The King's Road runs the length of the valley beside the river.

### Greywater (the river port)

The only real town: about four thousand people behind a stone wall, where the Senn is deep enough for barges. Warehouses, a fish market, three bridges, a guildhall, and the empty Judge's House that nobody has dared to move into. Run by the **Wharf Council**: seven seats, held by the guilds and two old families, the Marrens and the Tolls, who have hated each other for four generations. Everything that leaves the Senn Vale leaves through Greywater, so everyone has a reason to come here and a reason to resent it. The town's great bell, Old Senna, was recast after the flood of thirty years ago and has rung a little flat ever since. The Greywater fair fills the town for a week at the end of summer.
- Feel: crowded, wet, clever. Favours, debts, ledgers and gossip.

### The Tollhills (the hill farms)

Rolling sheep country west of the river, two days from Greywater. Scattered steadings, drystone walls, and a dozen hamlets that share a fair, a mill and a feud. Half the families are halflings, who were here before the kingdom and keep the best records of anyone: births, boundary stones and every insult since the first survey. The Tollhills take their name from the old toll stations on the hill roads, which now stand empty, or house a family, or an inn. The fair is the Quarter Fair, held four times a year on the green at Longwall, and the Fair Court sits there under the oak.
- Feel: pastoral, funny, stubborn. Everyone knows everyone. Small stakes, held very hard.

### Brannock's Cut (the mining town)

A steep town built into a gorge in the eastern hills, three days from Greywater. Silver and tin. Founded by dwarves, who still own the deep workings and the smelters. Humans work the upper galleries for wages and do not love the arrangement. About three hundred families live on those wages. The Cut is loud, smoky, rich in coin and poor in daylight. The **Delvers' Moot** (the dwarven owners) and the **Pick and Lamp** (the human miners' lodge) govern it by glaring at each other. The lowest level, the Ninth, was sealed by the dwarves sixty years ago. They say it flooded.
- Feel: hard work, hard bargains, pride. Grim, with gallows humour.

### The Hushwood (the deep forest)

Old forest covering the whole north of the Senn Vale. The outer wood is managed: charcoal burners, hunters, a few woodcutters' hamlets. Past the boundary oaks it belongs to others. A small elven community, the **Leaf-wardens**, lives deep inside and sends someone to the Greywater fair once a year to trade and say very little. The goblin clans live deeper still. They raid in hard winters and trade in good ones, and they are people, with names, grudges and a sense of humour, not a plague. The forest is quiet in a way visitors notice at once: birdsong carries strangely, and stops short.
- Feel: eerie, beautiful, patient. Exploration and the unknown.

### The Sallows (the fen)

Where the Senn spreads out and loses itself, four days south-east. Reed beds, black water, eel weirs, and villages on stilts and hummocks joined by plank walks. The fen folk are insular and generous in equal measure, and they have their own calendar, their own saints and their own words for forty kinds of mud. Outsiders get lost and are usually found. The fen folk do not go out after dark in the last week of autumn, and do not discuss why with strangers. They call it the Shut Week.
- Feel: fog, lamplight, superstition. Dread that builds slowly.

### The High Stair (the ruins)

On the plateau above the valley's head, five days from Greywater, stand the largest Builder ruins anyone knows: terraces, roofless halls, and a great stair cut into the cliff that climbs three hundred feet and ends at nothing. Shepherds graze the lower terraces. Treasure hunters come every few years and leave with little, or do not leave. A handful of scholars and a larger handful of cranks camp there in summer. Every part of the Senn Vale has some Builder stone in it, but this is where they lived, if they lived anywhere.
- Feel: wind, scale, silence. Old-fashioned delving and wonder.

## The peoples

- **Humans** are most of the population everywhere except the deep workings of the Cut and the deep Hushwood. Vale folk are practical, talkative, and suspicious of anyone who says they are from the crown.
- **Halflings** are the old people of the Tollhills, and a quarter of Greywater. Famous for memory, hospitality and lawsuits.
- **Dwarves** own Brannock's Cut. Few live anywhere else. They keep their word exactly, and they keep their own counsel about the deep levels.
- **Elves** are few: the Leaf-wardens of the Hushwood, and a handful of wanderers. They remember the Senn Vale before the road. They find humans' short memories sad, and useful.
- **Goblins** are neighbours, not vermin. Clan folk of the deep forest, with a long and mostly justified grievance about where the border oaks were planted. A goblin in Greywater gets stared at and served.
- **Orcs** come down from the northern heights in small bands as drovers, mercenaries and caravan guards. Blunt, honest in a contract, and tired of being asked if they are raiders.
- Anyone else is a traveller, and will be talked about for a month.

## Faith

There is no great church. The Senn Vale keeps **small gods and local saints**, each with a shrine, a feast day and a few people who take them seriously. A cleric here is a person with one god's attention, which is rarer and stranger than belonging to a church.

| Name | Cares for | Shrines |
|---|---|---|
| **The Lantern-Keeper** | Travellers, thresholds, anyone out after dark | A lamp niche at every crossroads and town gate |
| **Mother Furrow** | Harvest, births, bread | Every farm kitchen in the Tollhills |
| **The Drowned Saint** | The river, mercy, the lost | Greywater's bridges, and every stilt village in the Sallows |
| **Old Anvil** | Craft, oaths, fair dealing | The Cut. Dwarves swear contracts on a struck anvil |
| **The Quiet** | Death, rest, endings | Grave gates. Nobody prays to the Quiet for anything but peace |

The fen folk also keep a dozen saints of their own, and will not list them for outsiders. The elves of the Hushwood do not speak of gods at all.

## Power, law and money

- **Nobody rules the Senn Vale.** Greywater has its Wharf Council, the Cut has its Moot and its lodge, the Tollhills have a Fair Court that meets four times a year, the Sallows have their elders. The crown's law still stands in name, and people quote it when it suits them.
- **There is no army.** Greywater has forty paid watchmen. Every other place has a militia of whoever owns a spear. Serious trouble is handled by hiring someone, which is where adventurers come in.
- **Coin** is the kingdom's: copper, silver and gold. New coin has not come north in nine years, so old coin is worn thin and the Cut's silver bars pass by weight.
- **Travel** is by the King's Road or the river. An inn every day's walk on the road. Off the road, a farmhouse will take you in for news and an evening's work.

## Magic

- Magic is real, rare and a little distrusted. A village might have one person with a knack: a healer, a weather-eye, a finder of lost things.
- A **wizard** is an educated person with books, and there are perhaps a dozen in the Senn Vale, mostly in Greywater. People are polite to them and do not invite them to weddings.
- Magic items exist and are mostly old. The Builders made things that still work, and nobody knows how.
- Magic is not illegal, and not comfortable. Do it in a market square and the square goes quiet.

## Monsters and the wild

- The settled land is safe by day. Wolves, boar and the odd bandit are the usual troubles.
- The wild places are genuinely dangerous: the deep Hushwood, the far Sallows, the hill caves, the Builder ruins, the old workings of the Cut. Things live there that do not come out, and people leave them alone.
- The dead mostly stay dead. When they do not, it is a scandal and a terror, and people send for a priest of the Quiet.
- Every monster in the rules has a place here if it has a reason to be there. A monster is never just there. Something brought it, woke it, or drove it out of where it belongs.

## Things people say (rumour, not fact)

The DM can put any of these in the mouth of an innkeeper, a carter or a child. They are what people say. Whether any of them is true is not in this file.

- The grey milestones on the King's Road are older than the road, and they are never cold, not even in snow.
- The crown did not forget the Senn Vale. Something happened in Highcarrow, and nobody who knows will say.
- There is a children's counting rhyme known in every village, with nine lines. Everyone agrees there used to be ten.
- The dwarves did not seal the Ninth because it flooded.
- Old Senna rings flat because the bell founder put something in the bronze.
- You can walk into the Hushwood for three days, and walk out again in one.
- The halflings' boundary records go back further than the halflings admit.
- In the Sallows, the water level has not changed in thirty years, flood or drought.
- Somebody in Greywater is still getting letters from the capital.

## Names

Keep names plain, short and easy to say aloud.
- **Human** Vale folk: given names such as Aldith, Bram, Corwen, Desta, Hob, Ilsa, Joss, Merrin, Oswin, Tamsin, Wren. Family names from work or place: Carter, Marren, Reedmoor, Stonehand, Toll, Weir.
- **Halflings** add a steading to the name: Pella of Longwall, Dob of Three Stiles.
- **Dwarves** take a craft-name at adulthood: Brannoch Deepseam, Hild Truestrike.
- **Elves** use a single name and a season: Seren of the late frost.
- **Goblins** use short sharp names and a clan: Skrit of the Bent Oak.
- **Places** are descriptive: Greywater, Longwall, the Eel Stair, Three Stiles, Coldharbour, Cinder Row, Lantern Hythe.

## What the DM must keep consistent

- The six places, their distances and who runs them.
- The five gods and what each cares for.
- The crown is silent, not fallen, and nobody in the Senn Vale knows why.
- Goblins, orcs and the fen folk are people with their own interests.
- The Builders are unknown. Never explain them from this file.
- Anything new the DM invents for a campaign (an inn, a family, a feud) goes in that campaign's `world.md`, and must not contradict this file.
