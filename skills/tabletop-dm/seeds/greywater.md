# Greywater

DM only.

## Tone

Intrigue. Favours, ledgers, and who knows what. Violence is rare, quick and always witnessed. Everyone is polite, and almost nobody tells the whole truth.

## Start place

Low Bridge, Greywater, at the foot of the bell tower, which shares a wall with the empty Judge's House. Noon, market day, light rain.

## The opening scene

Old Senna is striking noon, flat as ever, when the front door of the Judge's House opens for the first time anyone can remember. Four watchmen carry out a body on a shutter. The crowd knows the boots: Hob Tallow, master chandler, warden of the bell, seventh seat on the Wharf Council. Within a minute a Marren docker and a Toll docker are shouting "murder" at each other, with the hero between them. In the crush a runner presses a sealed letter into the hero's hand, sees the wrong face, and bolts. The seal is the crown's. Captain Desta Stonehand, walking beside the shutter, has noticed, and she is already coming over.

- **When:** noon on day 1 of the count. The counter reads 10.
- **Before you start:** run the companion pitches first. Then ask the player, in one line, why the hero is here. Do not invent the reason for them.

## What is really going on here

The Council splits three and three, Tolls against Marrens. Hob was the swing. His seat is filled by vote in ten days, and the winner controls the harbour dues.

Hob was not murdered. Since the last tax riders, nine years ago, Tamsin Toll, who farms the dues, has gone on collecting the crown's share and kept it. Hob got her second ledger and meant to read it to the Council. Tamsin sent Gil Carter, a big soft-voiced wharf tough, to fetch it back. Hob fled up the tower and through the connecting door, Gil followed, and in the dark Hob fell down the Judge's stair and broke his neck. Gil ran, and has not managed to climb a stair since. The ledger was already hidden in the bell frame. One person saw it all: the ringer.

**Who knows what.**
- Tamsin: knows Hob fell, that Gil was there, and what the ledger holds. Wrongly believes the ledger is still in the Judge's House. Suspects Corwen Pell saw something from next door.
- Gil Carter: knows how Hob died. Wrongly believes nobody saw. Suspects Tamsin will let him carry it alone. (talk +1, notice +0)
- Joss: knows only his own shameful alibi. Wrongly believes a Toll blade killed Hob. Suspects Tamsin by name, with no proof.
- Captain Stonehand: knows the neck broke in a fall, and that the connecting door was opened from the tower side. Wrongly believes Hob died for the crown letter. Suspects both families, and the hero.
- Brun: knows the dues he is quoted never match the barges he counts. Wrongly believes the whole Council skims together. Suspects nothing about Hob.
- Corwen: knows the letter is hers and the runner was hers. Wrongly believes Hob came through the door to pry into her files. Suspects the hero has read the letter.
- Wren: knows all of it, and where the ledger is. Wrongly believes telling is useless, since nobody keeps what she says. She has told the watch twice already.

## The local villain

**Tamsin Toll**, sixty, head of the Tolls, never raises her voice. She wants the seat for her nephew, and the ledger. She thinks she is right because the crown's share has paid the watch, mended the river wall and bought grain in two bad winters, where the Council would have squabbled it away. Her receipts are real. She did not want Hob dead, and she sleeps badly. (talk +6, notice +5)
- Next: a Marren knife is "found" in the Judge's House, Joss Marren is arrested, two guild votes are bought.
- Cornered: she first offers an honest bargain, the fund made public under her management. Refused, she burns the ledger's twin and lets Gil carry the blame alone.

## Other people

- **Joss Marren**, the Marren heir. Loud, generous, unguarded. Wants to be seen avenging Hob, his godfather. His split knuckles are from losing a fight to a bargeman that night: his whole alibi, and he is ashamed of it. (talk +2, notice +0)
- **Captain Desta Stonehand** of the watch. Clipped, tired. Wants a culprit before the bridges riot, preferably the right one. Writes everything on a slate and wipes it nightly. (talk +4, notice +5)
- **Brun Ashweigh**, dwarf silver factor from the Cut. Dry, exact. Wants steady dues, whoever holds them. Weighs a coin in his palm before answering anything. (talk +3, notice +4)
- **Corwen Pell**, keeper of the Judge's House keys. Sixty, kindly, a clerk to the bone. Wants the house shut and her letter back. Ink on her right cuff only. She dates what she says: "as of this morning". (talk +5, notice +4)
- **Wren**, the bell-ringer, who lives in the tower loft. Patient, surprised by nothing. Nobody remembers hiring her, or remembers her ten minutes after speaking to her. She wants someone to know her name tomorrow. Companions and witnesses forget her. The hero keeps her only while the player does something to hold on: a written note, a name said each morning. Do not explain why. (talk +0, notice +5)

## Companion candidates

- **Merrin Carter**, Rogue. "A dockside runner who knows every back stair and who owes whom. She is quick and nosy, and she wants Hob's killer found before the wrong man swings."
  - Private: Gil is her brother. She wants him safe and hides that he was there. She leaves, or warns him, if the hero moves to hand him over without a deal. (talk +4, notice +3)
- **Brother Oswin**, Cleric of the Drowned Saint. "He keeps the lamp at the Low Bridge shrine, and he blessed Hob's body on the shutter. He is gentle and stubborn, and he wants the dead man given the truth as well as a prayer."
  - Private: Tamsin's charity pays for his shrine, and he hides how much. He leaves if the hero frames anyone or lies under oath. (talk +2, notice +3)
- **Sella Quill**, Wizard. "A young scrivener who audits guild ledgers and reads people nearly as well. She is curious past the point of manners, and she badly wants to see inside the Judge's House."
  - Private: Corwen Pell pays her to report who asks about the bell. She wants the judge's library. She reports on the hero until she is shown the Chancery letters. Then she changes sides. (talk +1, notice +5)

## If the hero does nothing

Deadline: 10 days, to the Council vote. Day 1 is the day of the opening scene. Before the opening scene, start it as a SECRET counter: `track --name "days to the vote" --set 10 --secret`. Drop it by 1 at each dawn: `--add -1`. The day number is 11 minus the counter. The vote is at noon on day 10, when the counter reads 1. Each event below has happened once the counter reads that number or lower.

1. Counter 10 (day 1): the opening scene.
2. Counter 9 (day 2): the Marren knife is found.
3. Counter 7 (day 4): dockers riot on Middle Bridge.
4. Counter 5 (day 6): after dark, Gil takes a night barge south and is not seen again.
5. Counter 3 (day 8): Joss Marren is arrested.
6. Counter 1 (day 10): the vote. Tamsin's nephew is seated. The Tolls hold four seats of seven, the dues double by spring, and nobody ever climbs to look at the bell.

## Fights

Counts suit a party of two. With three, add one more of the smallest monster to any fight that has several. For a hero alone, halve the counts (never below one), and treat every lone monster and every climax as Deadly: give the warning and show the way round.

1. Wharf toughs sent to warn the hero off: bandit x2. Avoided by naming their paymaster aloud in public, or paying better.
2. The flooded undercroft of the Judge's House: giant-rat x2. Avoided by the dry stair at low water.
3. The judge's strongroom: animated-armor x1 (level 2 or above, and Deadly below it: the dents in the door are the warning), still obeying its last order to pass nobody without the seal. Avoided by showing a crown seal. The letter carries one.
4. Tamsin's fixer on the night barge: bandit-captain x1 and bandit x1 (level 3, a climax, Hard). Avoided with the ledger as leverage, or by bringing Captain Stonehand.

## Local secrets

1. Brother Oswin's shrine runs on Toll money.
2. Joss Marren's alibi is a lost fist fight.
3. The Tolls have kept the crown's share for nine years.
4. Hob fell. Gil was there, and Tamsin sent him.
5. Someone lives in the bell tower, and Hob paid her from his own purse.

## The way in to the larger mystery

- **Session 1 detail:** the letter, a Chancery reply to "The Keeper, Judge's House": "We regret that we can find no record of the matter you describe, nor of the district. Kindly state again where you are writing from."
- **R3:** with the ledger is Hob's note: "Not bronze. Flat because of these. Ask Weir." Thirty-one grey studs sit in the bell's lip. Ilsa Weir, asked kindly and alone, says what she did and why, and not how it works.
- **R2:** shown a stud, Brun Ashweigh goes grey. "Those were to stay in the coffer. Ask the Moot why the Ninth is shut. Do not say I sent you."
- **R4:** Wren, and Hob's warden book: thirty years of ringers' pay, with four names nobody can place.
- **Never R1.** Greywater shows what was done with the pegs, never what the stones are for. The hopeful half of the truth lies behind other doors.

## Ways it can end

- The ledger is read out. The silver becomes a town fund, the Marrens crow, and Tamsin keeps her house and loses her name.
- The bargain: Tamsin keeps the seat and opens the books. Better for the town, and it feels like losing.
- Bittersweet: the only proof of how Hob died is Wren's word, which no court can hold. It stands only if the hero writes it down and swears to it as their own. Wren goes back up the stair, thanked by nobody, and rings the hour.
