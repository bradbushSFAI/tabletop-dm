# The Sallows

DM only.

## Tone

Slow-building dread. Fog, lamplight, kindness and shut doors. For a long time nothing happens that could not be explained. Keep the horror at the edge of the lamp: a shape on the plank walk, a voice asking what year it is. The fen folk are warm and frightened, both.

## Start place

Lantern Hythe, a stilt village of thirty houses at the end of the plank road, deep in the Sallows beside the great Builder weir called the Eel Stair. Dusk, in thick fog, nine nights before the last week of autumn, which the fen folk call the Shut Week.

## The opening scene

The village is taking in its plank walks, section by section toward the middle, nine nights earlier than custom. Nobody will say why. A punt drifts out of the fog and bumps the landing beside the hero. It is empty. Its lamp is still lit, and a supper is laid out in it for one, untouched. A woman pushes through to the rail: Linnet Reedmoor, whose husband poled out to the weirs this morning in that punt. She asks who will go out with her. Doors are closing along the walk. The light is going.

- **When:** dusk on day 1. This is night 1 of the count. The counter reads 9.
- **Before you start:** run the companion pitches first. Then ask the player, in one line, why the hero is here. Do not invent the reason for them.

## What is really going on here

Three people have vanished from Lantern Hythe this month. The elders say the Shut Week has come early. That is nearly right. The Week is old: the last week of autumn, when the lost show at the edges. Once that meant one soul at a window in a generation. For thirty years there have been more each autumn, and this year they are early.

Thirty years ago the elders drove their heirloom, the Saint's Nail, into the post of the Eel Stair to hold the water after the flood. Ten nights ago Ansel Withy, the weir-keeper, pulled it out. His wife Maud slipped from everyone's mind six years ago. He alone kept her, by writing her name on his forearm every morning. An elder once said the Nail "keeps what it is driven for". He drove it into his own doorpost and said her name.

It half worked. Maud comes to his door each night now, and he can see her clearly. She cannot cross the threshold, and she cannot leave it. The water is loose and has begun to drop. Far out, the lost of thirty years are drawn toward the one kept thing in the fen, like moths. The vanished villagers met them in the fog and followed a face they nearly knew. They are lost, not dead. So far.

**Who knows what.**
- Ansel: knows he has the Nail, that Maud comes, and that the water drops because of him. Wrongly believes the right words would let her in. Suspects the vanishings are his doing, and does not look at it.
- Mother Sedge: knows the Nail is gone from the post, what it did, and who the saints are. Wrongly believes the harpy takes the vanished. Suspects Perrin, the stranger, of stealing the Nail.
- Linnet: knows Col's round of the weirs. Wrongly believes he is hurt or drowned. Suspects the elders know more than they say.
- Maud: knows her name and Ansel's. Wrongly believes it is still the year she slipped. Suspects nothing, least of all that she is the cause.
- Perrin: knows prices, and that Wick has come into silver. Wrongly believes it is all fen superstition.
- Old Jory: knows someone shares his table, and not who. Believes nothing is wrong.

## The local villain

**Ansel Withy**, fifty, keeper of the weirs. Gentle, exhausted and exact. He wants his wife kept. He thinks he is right because it is one woman's whole existence against a hand's depth of water, and because nobody else even knew she was gone. (talk +2, notice +4)
- Next: he wants the elders' list of saints' names, to learn how the keeping is said properly. He will steal it.
- Cornered: he pulls the Nail and poles for the far fen and the drowned chapel, with Maud following on the water's edge.

## Other people

- **Mother Sedge**, eldest of Lantern Hythe, keeper of the saints' names. Speaks in weather: "There's a wind in that question." Wants the village inside by dark and the Nail back. Counts the people in any room under her breath before she will sit down. (talk +4, notice +5)
- **Linnet Reedmoor**, an eel-wife. Fierce, practical. Wants her husband Col found. He is alive on a hummock two miles out, reachable until dusk on night 3. Carries his second-best boots. (talk +3, notice +2)
- **Maud Withy**. Soft-voiced, polite. Asks what year it is and thanks you for telling her. Wants to come in, or to be let go. Her hem is dry, whatever the weather. (talk +1, notice +0)
- **Perrin Glim**, a lamp-oil pedlar stuck here until the road is safe. Cheerful, mercenary, the only one who jokes. Has doubled his prices and wants out before the Week. Tastes his own oil to prove that it is good. (talk +5, notice +3)
- **Old Jory Reedmoor**, Col's uncle, a net-mender. Slow, contented. Wants to be left to his evenings. Talks to the empty place at his table, and listens to it, and some nights he laughs. (talk +0, notice +1)

## Companion candidates

- **Wick**, Rogue. "An eel-poacher of nineteen who knows channels that the elders have forgotten. She is sour and quick, and she wants to be paid and gone before the Week."
  - Private: she saw Ansel at the Eel Stair on the night the water first dropped, and he bought her silence with Maud's silver ring. She hides the ring. She runs if accused in public before she can confess. (talk +4, notice +4)
- **Brother Alder**, Cleric of the Drowned Saint. "He keeps the stilt shrine and its lamp, and he will not let anyone go out alone after dark, which means that he goes himself. He is kind, tired and braver than he looks."
  - Private: his prayers for the lost come back as silence, and he no longer believes that the saint is listening. He hides it from the whole village. He leaves if the hero treats the lost as monsters, or despoils the chapel. (talk +2, notice +3)
- **Rafe Holloway**, Fighter. "A caravan guard from the Cut, wintering here to work off a debt to Mother Sedge. He thinks the fen is mad, and he will still stand wherever you put him."
  - Private: he cannot swim and is terrified of the water. He hides that under bluster. He leaves when the plank road goes, unless given a better reason than a debt. (talk +2, notice +1)

## If the hero does nothing

Deadline: 9 nights, to the Shut Week. Night 1 is the night of the opening scene. Before the opening scene, start it as a SECRET counter: `track --name "nights until the Shut Week" --set 9 --secret`. Drop it by 1 at each dawn: `--add -1`. The night number is 10 minus the counter. The Week begins at dusk on night 9, when the counter reads 1. Each event below has happened once the counter reads that number or lower.

1. Counter 9 (night 1): the opening scene.
2. Counter 8 (night 2): the water is down another hand. Eels die in the weirs.
3. Counter 7 (night 3): from dusk, Col Reedmoor can no longer be reached.
4. Counter 6 (night 4): a fourth villager vanishes, Mother Sedge's grown grandson.
5. Counter 4 (night 6): the plank road settles into the mud, and the village is cut off.
6. Counter 1 (night 9): the Week begins. The lost come in their dozens to stand on the walks outside Ansel's door. By the week's end the water breaks its old level for good, the stilts stand in drying mud, and Lantern Hythe is abandoned. Ansel and Maud are not seen again.

## Fights

Counts suit a party of two. With three, add one more of the smallest monster to any fight that has several. For a hero alone, halve the counts (never below one), and treat every lone monster and every climax as Deadly: give the warning and show the way round.

1. The stilts at night: giant-rat x2, driven up out of the falling water. Avoided by pulling the ladders and setting fire pots.
2. The old village site, uncovered by the drop: zombie x2. They are the flood's drowned, never laid to rest, walking toward the chapel bell. Avoided by ringing it for them, or with a rite of the Quiet.
3. The reed beds: harpy x1 (level 2). The falling water exposed her hummock, and she has moved close. The elders blame her for the vanishings, and they are wrong. Avoided with wax in the ears. She flees from fire.
4. The drowned chapel: ghoul x1 and zombie x2 (level 3, a climax, Hard). The ghoul has been at the opened graves. Avoided by coming at noon, when it lies up under the altar, and barring the crypt.

## Local secrets

1. Perrin waters his oil.
2. Wick has Maud's ring.
3. Every house lays a place for someone it has lost and cannot name. The fen's secret saints are those people: a name said on a feast day holds a little.
4. The harpy took nobody.
5. Ansel has the Nail. Maud stands at his door every night.

## The way in to the larger mystery

- **Session 1 detail:** every house in Lantern Hythe lays one more place at supper than it has people. Nobody eats from it. Asked why, they say "for the saint" and talk about something else.
- **R1:** the Eel Stair's post is Builder stone with one small empty socket, and the water that held for thirty years is falling. Mother Sedge: "What is counted is kept. We counted the water."
- **R4:** Maud, and the crowd in the fog. The saints' list is a list of neighbours.
- **R3:** Mother Sedge remembers a bell founder from Greywater, Ilsa Weir, who came the winter after the flood to ask how the keeping is done, and whether it could be turned round. "We told her. The next spring the bell rang flat. That autumn the first of ours went, and more every year since."
- **Never R5.** Nobody in the fen has ever thought about the elves.

## Ways it can end

- The Nail goes back into the post. The water steadies. Maud fades from the door, and Ansel is left with ink on his arm.
- The Nail is lost in the far fen, and the village moves to higher mud.
- Bittersweet: the Nail goes back, and the village takes Maud among its saints. Her name is said at every feast. On those days, and only those, she can be seen at the end of the walk, and Ansel is there early with two cups.
