# The Tollhills

DM only.

## Tone

Comic and pastoral. The stakes are a ram, a ribbon, a pie and a wedding, and the people in it would die for any of them. Play it straight-faced and let it be funny. The wrong note arrives late, and it should land like a dropped plate.

## Start place

The green at Longwall, on the first morning of the Quarter Fair. Stalls, a pie tent, the Fair Court's long table under the oak, and the grey Longwall Stone in the middle of the green with children climbing on it.

## The opening scene

Something large is coming through the fair at a gallop, wearing someone's washing. It is Magistrate: the biggest ram in the Tollhills, this year's ribbon winner, and the most valuable animal anyone here has seen. Behind him run two halfling families, with Nettie of Crook Stile and Hamo of Low Barn at their heads, both shouting that he is theirs. Ahead of him is the pie tent, with the judging table laid and the hero beside it. Aldith Grist, the miller, is already taking bets.

- **When:** the first morning of the fair, day 1 of the count. The counter reads 7.
- **Before you start:** run the companion pitches first. Then ask the player, in one line, why the hero is here. Do not invent the reason for them.

## What is really going on here

Magistrate turned up on the common in spring with an ear-notch no family could place. Nettie of Crook Stile fed him. Hamo of Low Barn sheared him. Each claims him, his stud fees would keep a steading for years, and the Fair Court hears the case in seven days. Hamo's son is due to marry Nettie's niece the day after. Both families have started saying "if".

The ram belongs to Underlea: five households in a fold of the hills a mile past Crook Stile, which slipped out of everyone's mind eleven years ago. Its people still farm there. They come to the fair and nobody serves them. Their ear-notch is in the rolls, on a page the recorder's eye slides past. Sheep and dogs are not fooled, which is why Magistrate keeps going home.

**Who knows what.**
- Hamo: knows he moved the stones and improved the roll. Wrongly believes the land is empty and the ram a stray. Suspects Wat is going soft on the feud.
- Nettie: knows the ram never stayed a week in her pen. Wrongly believes Hamo lures him away. Suspects Hamo's roll entry, and cannot say why.
- Tobin: knows the rolls come out five households wrong every year. Wrongly believes it is his own arithmetic. Suspects the ink on Hamo's entry is fresh.
- Aldith: knows she grinds more grain each quarter than twelve hamlets bring her. Wrongly believes her tally boy is careless. Suspects Wat of the open gate, and has odds on it.
- Posy: knows Wat is hiding something. Wrongly believes it is cold feet. Suspects nothing about the land.
- Ila: knows all of it: the notch, the stones, the fold. Wrongly believes nothing can be done. Suspects Bramble half remembers her.
- The rest of Underlea: know they are overlooked. Wrongly believe the hills are shunning them over some old offence.

## The local villain

**Hamo of Low Barn**, sixty, a shrine-warden sort of halfling, with a fine waistcoat and a verdict on everything. For ten years he has moved his boundary stones a yard a year into "the land nobody owns", which is Underlea's pasture. He wants the ram and the land. He thinks he is right because idle land is a sin, and because Tollhills custom gives land to whoever has used it unchallenged for ten years. By that custom he is right. (talk +4, notice +2)
- Next: he claims both at the Fair Court under the ten-year rule, with an entry in the rolls that he has improved a little.
- Cornered: he blusters, then produces the forged entry. If he is shown Underlea, he stops. His sister Ila married into Underlea, and he had forgotten he had a sister.

## Other people

- **Nettie of Crook Stile**, the rival claimant and six-time pie champion. Speaks in judgments: "That's a poor hat." Wants to beat Hamo at anything at all. Flour in her eyebrows at every hour. (talk +3, notice +2)
- **Tobin of Longwall**, recorder of the Fair Court, eighty and precise. Wants the rolls to balance, and they never quite do. He licks his thumb and turns two pages at once, always in the same place, and does not know it. (talk +1, notice +3)
- **Aldith Grist**, the miller, human, bored and sharp. Runs a book on everything and wants the case to last. Her odds are chalked on the mill door. (talk +4, notice +5)
- **Posy of Crook Stile**, the bride. Cheerful, out of patience. Wants to be married on the day, feud or no feud. She has already moved her hope chest to Low Barn by night, in a wheelbarrow. (talk +2, notice +1)
- **Ila of Underlea**, Hamo's sister. Quiet, amused, long past crying about it. Wants her brother to look at her. She leaves cheeses on Longwall doorsteps, unsigned, and people thank the wrong neighbour. (talk +0, notice +4)

## Companion candidates

- **Wat of Low Barn**, Fighter. "The groom: a large, gentle lad with the militia's second-best spear. He wants this feud finished before his wedding, and he will carry anything you ask him to."
  - Private: Wat let the ram out on fair morning, hoping that a lost ram would end the quarrel. He hides it badly. He leaves if the hero shames his father in front of the whole fair. (talk -1, notice +0)
- **Bramble**, Rogue. "A halfling odd-jobber and occasional poacher who knows every gap in every wall. She is good company and she wants the finder's fee."
  - Private: for years she has traded with "the folk in the fold" and cannot recall their faces afterwards. She keeps a tally stick of bargains she does not remember making, and it scares her. She leaves if the hero brings the Fair Court's bailiffs down on Underlea. (talk +3, notice +4)
- **Mother Orla**, Cleric of Mother Furrow. "A hearty travelling blesser of kitchens, who has vowed to bless every hearth in the hills before the harvest ends. She has opinions on your boots and a blessing for your bread."
  - Private: her count of kitchens never comes out the same twice. She wants to know why and dreads the answer. She leaves if the hero mocks the vow, or lets any hearth go cold that could have been kept. (talk +3, notice +2)

## If the hero does nothing

Deadline: 7 days, to the Fair Court hearing. Day 1 is the day of the opening scene. Before the opening scene, start it as a SECRET counter: `track --name "days to the Fair Court" --set 7 --secret`. Drop it by 1 at each dawn: `--add -1`. The day number is 8 minus the counter. The Court sits at noon on day 7, when the counter reads 1. The wedding is set for the day after. Each event below has happened once the counter reads that number or lower.

1. Counter 7 (day 1): the opening scene.
2. Counter 6 (day 2): the prize ribbon is stolen. Each family blames the other.
3. Counter 5 (day 3): the wedding is called off, loudly, by the wrong people.
4. Counter 3 (day 5): after dark, rustlers hired by Hamo take Magistrate, to hold him safe until the hearing.
5. Counter 1 (day 7): the Court awards the ram, and "the vacant land beyond Crook Stile", to Hamo. He walls it in spring. Fenced out of their own fields, with no neighbour's claim left to hold them, the people of Underlea slip entirely by winter.

## Fights

Counts suit a party of two. With three, add one more of the smallest monster to any fight that has several. For a hero alone, halve the counts (never below one), and treat every lone monster and every climax as Deadly: give the warning and show the way round.

1. The mill's grain store: giant-rat x2. Aldith insists they are large mice. Avoided with the mill cat, which she has locked out over a grudge.
2. The common at night, after the ram: wolf x2. Avoided with fire and noise. They are thin and wary.
3. The rustlers at the ford: bandit x4 (level 2). They are from outside the hills and terrified of halfling lawsuits. Avoided by serving them with one.
4. The pack in Underlea's fold: wolf x2 and dire-wolf x1 (level 3, a climax, Hard). Nobody hunts wolves on land nobody remembers, so they have bred there. Avoided by driving the flock in and lighting the old beacon.

## Local secrets

1. Wat let the ram out.
2. Posy has already moved in.
3. Hamo moves his boundary stones, and has improved the rolls.
4. The cheeses, and Bramble's tally stick.
5. Underlea.

## The way in to the larger mystery

- **Session 1 detail:** by custom the green is pegged out for thirteen pitches. There are twelve hamlets. The thirteenth pitch always stands empty, and nobody has ever asked why. Today someone has left a cheese on it.
- **R4:** Underlea itself, and Hamo's face when he sees his sister.
- **R1:** Tobin's oldest rolls are rubbings of marks cut into the Longwall Stone, which is warm under the children's hands. Each hamlet once "beat its bounds" at the fair by touching its mark and saying its name aloud. Underlea's bound-beater, old Marl the recorder, died twelve years ago, and no one took it up. The mark is still there. The children on the stone count each other out with the old rhyme, and every one of them pauses after the ninth line, as if someone else were due to finish it.
- **Never R2.** Nothing in the Tollhills points to the Cut. Here the mystery is green and open to the sky.

## Ways it can end

- Hamo loses. Magistrate goes home. Underlea's bounds are beaten again at every fair, by the whole green, shouting. It holds, faintly. The wedding goes ahead with thirteen tables.
- Hamo wins, and the wall goes up.
- Bittersweet: the case is won and the pie is eaten, and by the next morning the hero is the only one who still knows Ila's name. Hamo has chalked "I have a sister" on his own barn door, where he will see it every day.
